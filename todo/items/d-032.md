## Why not a post every day

The owner asked (2026-11-09) for a daily Nextdoor post "for the time being to get more views". Nextdoor's
own guidance says otherwise: "aim to share a new post every two weeks", because "post engagement generally
declines after about two weeks", and reactions and replies bump a post back up the feed
(https://business.nextdoor.com/en-us/blog/5-steps-to-success-with-nextdoor-business-posts, updated
2024-01-31). An older Nextdoor FAQ (2019) capped business posts at two a month. Daily posts would compete
with each other and risk the page being muted, so the daily part is a five-minute check instead.
The owner then chose weekly while the page is new (below).

## Every day (5 minutes)

- Reply to any comment or message on the business page, the same day. Each reply bumps the post again.
- Look for neighbours in the area asking for a driving instructor. Ask a happy pupil or parent to answer
  and recommend her; that does more than any post.
- After a pupil passes: ask them (and a parent) to recommend her on Nextdoor (footer link on the site).

## Posts: Nextdoor weekly while the page is new, then every two weeks; Google every week

- **Nextdoor:** weekly for the first four weeks (owner, 2026-11-09: "once a week ... cause it is completely
  new on nextdoor"), then every two weeks from December. If Nextdoor refuses a post (its 2019 rule allowed
  two business posts a month), that is the limit: go to every two weeks straight away.
- **Google profile:** BLOCKED until the profile is verified (d-018; 2026-11-09 it said "No more ways to
  verify"). Once verified: every week, the same text as that week's post (owner asked how often is feasible on
  Google). Google sets no limit and posts stay for six months before they are archived
  (https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169). Leave the phone number out of Google posts (Google
  may reject a number it can't link to the business) and add a "Learn more" button to
  https://seymadrivingschool.com/ (WhatsApp first there; "Call now" would often ring mid-lesson). Google
  can schedule posts ahead, so several can be written in one go.
